## Week-by-week in-class read-together

### Week 1 — Introduction to UCD

**Read:** `Myers1998_BriefHistoryHCI.pdf` **pages 1–7**

**What you will notice:** Timeline of HCI research → products; direct manipulation, mouse, windows, hypertext and early multimedia — the lineage digital media still inherits.

**Discuss:** Name one streaming / learning / social product and which historical interaction style it inherits. Rewrite a weak product brief into a people-under-constraint problem.

---

### Week 2 — Foundations of UCD

**Read:** `ISO9241-210_2019_sample.pdf` **pages 6–7, 11–13** (skip copyright/TOC filler on earlier pages)

**What you will notice:** Human-centred design as a lifecycle stance; Scope; rationale; §5 principles (users throughout, evaluation-driven design, whole UX).

**Discuss:** Map each principle onto one arc of the UCD cycle (research → design → evaluate). Where would a digital-media team skip a step and still claim “user-centred”?

---

### Week 3 — HCI principles in digital culture

**Read:** `Nielsen1994_EnhancingExplanatoryPowerUsabilityHeuristics.pdf` **pages 1–7** (whole paper)

**What you will notice:** How usability heuristics were refined; appendix lists of principles — visibility, feedback, consistency, and related critique language.

**Discuss:** For one media screen: what is visible, what feedback arrives, what is afforded or blocked? Which heuristic fails first?

---

### Week 4 — Digital accessibility and inclusive design

**Read:** `Microsoft_InclusiveDesign_Manual.pdf` **pages 8–14**

**What you will notice:** Inclusive design principles intro + **Recognize exclusion** — disability as mismatch at the point of interaction; temporary and situational exclusion.

**Discuss:** Can a product “pass a checklist” and still exclude? Give one digital-media example (captions, motion, contrast, time limits).

---

### Week 5 — User research methods

**Read:** `DOE2017_UsabilityGuidebook.pdf` **pages 2–3, 8–10**

**What you will notice:** Defining measurable goals; interviews and focus groups as generative methods (purpose, recruiting, session design).

**Discuss:** Pick one decision you must make next week. Is interview/focus group the right method — or would observation / survey / usability test fit better?

---

### Week 6 — Survey design

**Read:** `DOE2017_UsabilityGuidebook.pdf` **pages 4–5**

**What you will notice:** Survey pros/cons; writing and recruiting guidance (attitudes at scale, not behaviour).

**Discuss:** Rewrite one double-barrelled or leading item. Name the bias and how a small pilot would catch it.

---

### Week 7 — Personas, journeys, and problem statements

**Read:** `DOE2017_UsabilityGuidebook.pdf` **pages 13–17**

**What you will notice:** Customer profiles & personas (evidence-based); scenarios as task/journey framing for later testing and design.

**Discuss:** Build one chain: research signal → persona pattern → scenario pain → problem statement *without* a smuggled feature.

---

### Week 8 — Ideation methods

**Read:** `Microsoft_ProductInclusionToolkit.pdf` **pages 1–5** (whole toolkit)

**What you will notice:** Inclusive Growth Doorways + 10 Product Inclusion Actions — concrete inclusion criteria for selecting ideas after diverge.

**Discuss:** Turn last week’s problem statement into 3 How Might We questions, then score ideas against problem fit, feasibility, **and** these inclusion actions — not sketch beauty.

---

### Week 9 — Wireframing fundamentals

**Read:** `DOE2017_UsabilityGuidebook.pdf` **pages 15–19**

**What you will notice:** Scenarios (content/tasks before chrome) + usability reviews on sketches, wireframes, and early prototypes.

**Discuss:** Draft wireframe labels as messages, not empty boxes. What would a review catch before hi-fi polish?

---

### Week 10 — High-fidelity prototyping

**Read:** `Egger2016_WaitingTimesQoEWebServices.pdf` **pages 2–7**

**What you will notice:** Waiting-time QoE; classic 0.1 / 1 / 10 s response limits; how delay shapes perceived interactivity and flow — relevant to micro-interactions and realistic feedback in hi-fi prototypes.

**Discuss:** Where does your hi-fi prototype need explicit system status? When does animation help perceived wait — and when is it noise?

---

### Week 11 — Information architecture

**Read:** `DOE2017_UsabilityGuidebook.pdf` **pages 23–29**

**What you will notice:** Card sorting (mental models for grouping) through creating IA after analysis, then tree-test setup (findability before screens).

**Discuss:** Why a closed card sort must not rubber-stamp an existing menu. What would a tree test catch that a sitemap sketch cannot?

---

### Week 12 — Usability testing

**Read together:**
1. `Nielsen2000_WhyFiveUsers.pdf` **pages 1–5**
2. `Nielsen_Landauer1993_FindingUsabilityProblems.pdf` **pages 1–2**

**What you will notice:** Why iterative rounds of ~5 users beat one giant study; opening of the mathematical model behind diminishing returns.

**Discuss:** Plan one round of 5 users → redesign → test again. Write non-leading tasks. When would you recruit *more* than five (distinct user groups)?
